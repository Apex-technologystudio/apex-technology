Most POS software sold today is cloud software: it runs on someone else's server and your shop reaches it through a browser. That model works well in places where the internet is boring — always on, rarely thought about.

Pakistan is not always that place.

## The difference in one line

**Cloud POS** stores your data on a remote server. No connection, no billing.

**Offline POS** is installed on the shop's own computer and stores data there. The connection is irrelevant to whether you can serve a customer.

## What actually happens when the connection drops

This is the question that decides it, so be specific when you ask a vendor:

> "The internet is down and there are six customers in the queue. What happens?"

With true offline software, nothing happens — you bill as normal. With cloud software, you cannot take a sale until the connection returns. Some cloud products offer an "offline mode" that queues transactions, which helps, but it is usually limited: product search, reports and stock levels often stop working even when billing does not.

Load-shedding compounds it. A power cut takes the router down with the PC, so a shop can be offline for hours at a stretch — and those hours are not always quiet ones.

## Where cloud genuinely wins

Be fair about this, because for some businesses cloud is the right answer:

- **Multiple branches** you want to see in one place, live
- **Checking today's sales from home** without going to the shop
- **No local backup discipline** — the provider handles it for you
- **Several people needing access** from different locations

If your business looks like that, and your connection is reliable, cloud is a reasonable choice.

## Where offline wins

- Billing never stops for a connection or a power cut
- No monthly fee — the software is bought once, not rented
- Your sales and customer data stay on your own machine
- Nothing to lose access to if you stop paying
- Faster in daily use: no round trip to a server for every search

## The honest trade-off with offline

Offline software puts backup responsibility on you. If the hard disk dies and there is no copy, the data is gone. Any offline POS worth buying should therefore back itself up automatically — Apex POS copies the database to a folder you choose every two hours, and we recommend pointing that at a USB drive or a synced cloud folder so there is a copy off the machine.

Multi-branch is also harder offline. If you run several shops that must share one live view of stock, be honest that offline is not the natural fit.

## A practical rule

Ask yourself one thing: **if the internet died right now, would you still be able to serve the person in front of you?**

If the answer must be yes, buy offline. If your business genuinely needs live multi-location access more than it needs uninterrupted billing, buy cloud.
