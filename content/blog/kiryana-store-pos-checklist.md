Most POS software is designed for a shop with a few hundred products, fixed prices and customers who pay at the counter. A kiryana store has thousands of items, sells some of them by weight, and runs a parallel credit business on the side.

Here is what to actually check before you buy.

## 1. Can you bill without touching the mouse?

At a busy counter, reaching for a mouse for every item is the difference between a moving queue and a stuck one. The whole sale — find item, quantity, price, add to cart — should be reachable from the keyboard, with a barcode scanner where you have barcodes.

Ask the vendor to bill five items in front of you and watch their hands.

## 2. Does it handle per-kilogram items?

Rice, pulses, sugar and produce are sold by weight. Software built only around per-unit items forces you into workarounds — creating a fake "1 kg" product and multiplying in your head — and workarounds are where stock counts go wrong.

You want products that can be set up **per unit or per kilogram** natively.

## 3. Is udhaar a real feature or an afterthought?

Ask specifically: can you see every outstanding customer balance on one screen, with a total?

Many products let you record a credit sale but give you no way to review credit as a whole, which is the part that actually matters. See [running udhaar without losing money](/blog/udhaar-management-for-shops) for what a working system needs.

## 4. Will it tell you what is running out?

A kiryana loses money in two directions: dead stock tying up cash, and fast-movers running out before anyone notices. You want a low-stock threshold you set yourself, and a count of items below it visible without running a report.

## 5. Can staff use it without seeing your margins?

If a worker runs the counter, they need to bill. They do not need to see what you paid for stock, your profit per item, or the monthly reports.

Check that staff accounts are genuinely separate, and that purchase prices and financials sit behind a different password.

## 6. Does it work when the internet does not?

Covered properly in [offline vs cloud POS](/blog/offline-vs-cloud-pos-pakistan), but for a kiryana the answer is nearly always offline. A general store cannot tell a queue to wait for a router.

## 7. What happens to your data if the computer dies?

Ask where backups go, how often, and how you restore one. "It backs up automatically" is not an answer — you want to know the folder, the interval, and the recovery steps.

## 8. Who enters your product list?

A kiryana catalogue is thousands of lines. Entering it is real work. Establish before you pay whether the vendor does it, whether they can import from a spreadsheet, and what it costs.

## The two questions that matter most

If you only ask two things:

1. **"Show me billing a sale from scratch, right now."** Speed at the counter is the feature you use ten thousand times a month.
2. **"Show me every customer who owes me money, on one screen."** That is the feature that decides whether the shop keeps its cash.

Everything else is negotiable.
