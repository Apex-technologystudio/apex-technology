Search for a POS system and you will be shown all-in-one terminals costing more than the software. Most shops do not need one.

Here is what actually earns its place.

## The computer

If your shop has a working **Windows 10 or 11** PC or laptop, that is very likely enough. POS software is not demanding — Apex POS is around 38 MB and runs comfortably on hardware that would struggle with modern web applications.

What matters more than speed:

- **It stays on during shop hours.** A machine someone else uses for other things is a bad choice.
- **It has a USB port free** for the scanner, and another for the printer.
- **It is not the machine your backups also live on.** A backup on the same disk is not a backup.

You do not need a touchscreen. It is pleasant, and genuinely faster in a restaurant where staff tap items rather than scan them, but for a retail counter a keyboard is faster.

## Thermal receipt printer

This is the one piece most shops should buy. Thermal printers use heat rather than ink, so there are **no cartridges to replace** — just rolls of paper. They are fast, quiet and cheap to run.

What to check:

- **80mm or 58mm paper.** 80mm is the common shop size and fits more per line; 58mm is narrower and cheaper to feed. Pick one and stay with it, because rolls are not interchangeable.
- **USB connection**, unless you specifically need network printing.
- **A Windows driver exists.** Ask the seller to confirm it. A printer without a working Windows driver is a paperweight.
- **Auto-cutter**, if the budget allows. Tearing every receipt by hand gets old quickly at volume.

## Barcode scanner

Worth it if your products carry barcodes — packaged goods, medicines, electronics. Less useful if you mostly sell loose or unbranded items, where typing two letters of the name is faster than hunting for a barcode that is not there.

What to check:

- **USB "plug and play"** scanners appear to Windows as a keyboard. They need no driver and work with any software that accepts typed input, including Apex POS.
- **1D is enough** for ordinary retail barcodes. 2D scanners also read QR codes and cost more; buy one only if you know you need QR.
- **Wired unless you have a reason.** Wireless adds charging to your daily routine for a device that sits in one place.

## Cash drawer

Optional, and genuinely useful mainly at higher volumes. Most connect through the receipt printer and open when a sale completes.

If you are starting out, skip it and add it later.

## What you can skip

- **All-in-one POS terminals.** A large cost for hardware you can assemble more cheaply, and harder to replace when one part fails.
- **Customer-facing displays.** Rare in Pakistani retail and rarely missed.
- **Tablets**, if your software is a Windows application. Apex POS is desktop software: it runs on your PC, not on a tablet.

## A sensible starting setup

For most shops: **the PC you already have, one 80mm thermal printer, one USB 1D barcode scanner.** Add a cash drawer when volume justifies it.

That combination handles a busy kiryana, a pharmacy or a mini mart without difficulty.
