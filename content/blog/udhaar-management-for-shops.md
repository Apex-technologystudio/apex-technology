Almost every shop in Pakistan runs on some amount of udhaar. It is not a flaw in how business is done here — extending credit to a regular customer is what keeps them a regular customer.

The problem is never the credit. It is not knowing the size of it.

## The question most shopkeepers cannot answer instantly

**How much money are you owed right now, in total?**

Most shopkeepers know their biggest debtors by name. Very few can state the total across everyone, or say which balances have been sitting untouched for three months. The register holds the information, but you cannot ask a register a question — you can only read it, page by page.

That gap is where the loss lives. Money owed for six months is not really owed any more; it is money lost that nobody has written down as lost.

## What a working system needs

Whether you do this on paper or in software, it needs four things:

**1. Every credit sale attached to a person.** A name is not enough. A name and a phone number, recorded at the moment of sale, not reconstructed later.

**2. One running total per customer.** Not a list of transactions you have to add up under pressure while the person is standing in front of you.

**3. An age on every balance.** A PKR 5,000 balance from last week and one from last year are completely different problems. If you cannot see which is which, you will treat them the same.

**4. A total across everyone.** This is the number that tells you how much of your working capital is currently sitting in other people's homes.

## Chasing it without damaging the relationship

The reason balances get old is rarely that a customer refuses to pay. It is that nobody asked, because asking felt awkward and there was no natural moment for it.

A few things that work:

- **Follow up early.** A polite reminder at three weeks is a normal business conversation. At six months it is a confrontation. The longer you wait, the harder it gets and the less likely you are to do it.
- **Make it about the record, not the person.** "Bhai, meray record mein aap ka 4,000 pending hai" is easier to say, and easier to hear, than a personal demand.
- **Set a quiet limit.** Decide privately how much credit any one customer gets before you pause. You do not need to announce it — you just need to know it before it is exceeded.
- **Confirm the amount at the time of sale.** Most disputes are honest disagreements about how much, not refusals to pay. Both parties agreeing at the moment of purchase prevents nearly all of them.

## Where software genuinely changes things

Software does not chase anyone for you. What it does is remove the excuse that you did not know.
